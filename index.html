Do richer countries have less inequality?

Simon Kuznets argued in 1955 that inequality rises as a country starts to industrialize, then falls once it gets rich. That predicts an inverted U. A weak downward slope

Across recent surveys, Gini falls slightly as income rises. The correlation with log GDP per capita is about −0.27, so income explains less than a tenth of the variation. Countries at the same income level can differ by 25 Gini points or more.

Region matters more than income

Highlight Sub-Saharan Africa or Latin America and every country above a Gini of 45 in recent surveys lights up, from poor to upper-middle income. Europe's rich and middle-income countries sit at the bottom of the chart. Much of the "hump" in the curve comes from where those two groups fall on the income axis.

Read the low values with care

Most of Asia and Africa measure inequality in consumption, while Latin America and rich countries mostly measure income. Consumption is spread more evenly than income, so those countries can look more equal than they are. India's 2023 figure of 23 is a consumption-based number.
